Ratios, rates, and unit rates. Do one section a day, about 5 minutes. Set a timer and
stop when it rings.

## Start here: say the step out loud

For math, the trick isn't the answer — it's **knowing which move to make**. For each
problem:

1. **Name it:** is this a ratio, a rate, or a unit-rate (per one) problem?
2. **Say the move:** "multiply both parts," or "divide to get per one."
3. **Do it, then label the answer** with its units ($, pounds, minutes).

**Brain tip:** underline the two amounts and their labels first. A number with no label
is easy to mix up.

## Ratios: compare two amounts (order matters)

A ratio compares two amounts, like **3 red : 2 yellow**. Write them in the order the
question asks.

### Make an equivalent ratio: do the SAME thing to both parts

| Start | Move | Result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 2 : 3 | × 3 both parts | 6 : 9 |
| 10 : 15 | ÷ 5 both parts | 2 : 3 |

> **Tape diagram:** each box is worth the same amount. Find one box first, then multiply.
> *Cats : dogs = 4 : 5, 27 animals. 9 boxes → each box = 27 ÷ 9 = 3. Dogs = 5 × 3 = 15.*

## Rates: an amount "per" something

A rate pairs two different units: **miles per minute, dollars per hour**. To solve,
**divide to find the amount for ONE**, then multiply up.

| Problem | Per one | Then |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 32 miles in 160 min | 160 ÷ 32 = **5 min per mile** | 100 miles × 5 = 500 min |
| $58 in 8 hours | 58 ÷ 8 = **$7.25 per hour** | 3 hours × 7.25 = $21.75 |

## Unit rate & unit price (this is the one to nail)

A **unit rate** is the amount for exactly ONE. For shopping, that's the **price per
pound** (or per item). **Divide price ÷ amount.**

### Trap: "which is the better deal?" → compare price PER POUND, and LOWER wins

| Bag | Price ÷ pounds | Per pound |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A: 3 lb for $6 | 6 ÷ 3 | **$2.00** |
| B: 5 lb for $8 | 8 ÷ 5 | **$1.60** ← cheaper |

> **Say it back:** "Divide each one down to one pound. $1.60 is less than $2.00, so Bag B
> is the better deal."

**Watch the units.** "How many seconds?" means your answer must be in seconds — convert
minutes to seconds (× 60) if you worked in minutes.

## Converting units with ratios

A conversion fact **is a ratio**: 12 inches : 1 foot. So you can use a ratio table
(or a unit rate) to switch units.

| Fact | Ratio |
| --- | --- |
| 1 foot = **12** inches · 1 yard = **3** feet · 1 mile = **5,280** feet | length (customary) |
| 1 meter = **100** centimeters · 1 kilometer = **1,000** meters | length (metric) |
| 1 pound = **16** ounces · 1 kilogram = **1,000** grams | weight / mass |
| 1 inch ≈ **2.54** cm · 1 meter ≈ **3.28** feet | between systems |

| feet | 1 | 5 | 7 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **inches** | 12 | 60 | **84** |

> 7 feet = 7 × 12 = **84 inches**. Going the other way, 54 inches ÷ 12 = **4.5 feet**.

### Trap: multiply or divide? Do the size check

**Bigger unit → smaller number. Smaller unit → bigger number.**
Converting 3 meters to centimeters? Centimeters are tiny, so you need **more** of them:
3 × 100 = **300 cm**. If your answer got smaller, you divided by mistake.

### Unit price with a unit conversion: convert FIRST, then divide

To compare prices in different units, put them in the **same unit** first.

| Deal | Same unit | Per ounce |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 lb of trail mix for $8 | 1 lb = 16 oz → $8 ÷ 16 | **$0.50** |
| 12 oz for $5.40 | already ounces → $5.40 ÷ 12 | **$0.45** ← cheaper |

> **Say it back:** "I changed pounds to ounces so both are per ounce. $0.45 is less than
> $0.50, so the 12-ounce bag is the better deal."

## Double number line

Line up the two amounts and move them together. Whatever you multiply the top by,
multiply the bottom by the same.

```
dollars:  0    7.25   14.50   21.75
hours:    0     1       2       3
```

## Percentages (a rate out of 100)

A **percent** means "per 100." It's a rate where the whole is 100.

- **Percent → decimal:** move the point 2 places left. 20% = **0.20**.
- **Percent of a number:** multiply. 20% of 50 = 0.20 × 50 = **10**.
- **What percent is it?** part ÷ whole × 100. 15 out of 60 → 15 ÷ 60 = **25%**.

> Percents, fractions, and decimals are three ways to say the same thing:
> **½ = 0.5 = 50%.** (More percent work — tax, tips, discounts — is in the
> *Percent Increase & Decrease* lesson.)

## Write a strong answer (ACE)

Right answers still lose points when they're a bare number or a fragment. Show your
thinking the way every teacher wants — with **ACE**:

- **A — Answer** by restating the question in a complete sentence. *(Don't START with
  "Because." Restate first, then give your "because.")*
- **C — Calculate / show it** with **labels and units** — "$30," "20 square units,"
  "$1.20 per box" — not just "30."
- **E — Explain** the *why* with the rule or formula you used.

| Weak | Strong (ACE) |
| --- | --- |
| *30.* | *The area is 30 square units, because ½ × 10 × 6 = 30.* |
| *Yes.* | *Yes, 6:8 equals 3:4, because dividing both by 2 gives 3 and 4.* |
| *$10.* | *The sale price is $30, because 25% of $40 is $10 off, and $40 − $10 = $30.* |

> **Then check it:** reread the question and make sure you answered **what it actually
> asked** — not just the first number you found.

## Cheat card (cut out and keep by the homework)

|  | Ask yourself | Move |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Ratio** | Comparing two amounts? What order? | multiply/divide BOTH parts the same |
| **Rate** | Is it "per" something? | divide to get per ONE, then multiply up |
| **Unit Rate** | Price or amount for ONE? Better deal? | divide total ÷ amount · LOWER per-unit wins |

**Always label the answer with its units, and match what the question asked for.**

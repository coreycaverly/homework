**Unit 8.** Describing a pile of data with a few numbers: the **center** (typical value)
and the **spread** (how spread out it is), plus reading the graphs.

## Measures of center

| Measure | How to find it | Example: 3, 7, 7, 10, 13 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Mean** (average) | add them all, **divide by how many** | 40 ÷ 5 = **8** |
| **Median** (middle) | **put in order**, take the middle one | **7** |
| **Mode** (most) | the value that appears **most** | **7** |

> **Even count?** The median is the **average of the two middle numbers**.
> For 4, 6, 8, 10 → (6 + 8) ÷ 2 = **7**.

### Trap: order BEFORE finding the median

The median is the middle of the **sorted** list. 9, 2, 5 → sort to 2, 5, 9 → median **5**.

## Measures of spread

- **Range** = greatest − least (13 − 3 = **10**). How wide the data is.
- An **outlier** is a value far from the rest. It can pull the **mean** up or down a lot,
  but barely moves the **median** — so median is safer with outliers.

## Reading the graphs

| Graph | Shows |
| --- | --- |
| **Dot plot** | one dot per data point; tall stacks = common values |
| **Histogram** | bars for **ranges** of values (0–9, 10–19, …) |
| **Box plot** | the median, the middle half of the data, and the range |

A **statistical question** is one that **expects different answers** ("How tall are the
students?"), not one fixed answer ("How tall is Sam?").

## Cheat card

|  | Do this |
| --- | --- |
| **Mean** | add, divide by the count |
| **Median** | **sort**, take the middle (avg the two middles if even) |
| **Mode** | the most frequent value |
| **Range** | greatest − least |
| **Outlier** | far-off value; moves the mean, not the median |

## Practice

Data: 5, 8, 8, 9, 20.
1. Mean?   2. Median?   3. Mode?   4. Range?
5. Which measure of center does the 20 pull up the most — mean or median?

## STOP & CHECK

- ☐ I **sorted** the data before finding the median.
- ☐ For the mean, I divided by **how many** numbers there are.
- ☐ Range is greatest **minus** least.

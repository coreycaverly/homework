**Unit 3.** Dividing fractions, and adding, subtracting, multiplying, and dividing decimals.
Two big ideas: **"keep–change–flip"** for fractions, and **line up / count the places** for decimals.

## Dividing fractions

Dividing asks **"how many of these fit in that?"** To compute it, **multiply by the
reciprocal** (flip the second fraction):

> **Keep. Change. Flip.** Keep the first fraction, change ÷ to ×, flip the second.

| Problem | Keep–change–flip | Answer |
| --- | --- | --- |
| ½ ÷ ¼ | ½ × 4⁄1 | **2** (two ¼'s fit in ½) |
| ¾ ÷ ½ | ¾ × 2⁄1 = 6⁄4 | **1½** |
| 6 ÷ ⅓ | 6⁄1 × 3⁄1 | **18** |

**Reciprocal** = flip the fraction: the reciprocal of ¾ is 4⁄3. A whole number *n* is *n*⁄1.

### Trap: only flip the SECOND fraction

Keep the first one exactly as it is. Only the divisor (the one after ÷) flips.

## Decimals

**Add / subtract:** line up the **decimal points**, then add or subtract normally.
Fill empty spots with 0s.

```
  3.40
+ 1.07
------
  4.47
```

**Multiply:** ignore the points, multiply, then the answer has **as many decimal places as
both factors combined**.

> 0.3 × 0.4 → 3 × 4 = 12 → two decimal places → **0.12**

**Divide:** move the decimal in the **divisor** to make it a whole number, move the one in
the **dividend** the same number of places, then divide.

> 4.5 ÷ 0.5 → move both one place → 45 ÷ 5 = **9**

## Cheat card

|  | Do this |
| --- | --- |
| **Fraction ÷ fraction** | keep – change – flip, then multiply across |
| **Add/subtract decimals** | line up the decimal points |
| **Multiply decimals** | multiply, then count total decimal places |
| **Divide decimals** | make the divisor whole, move both the same |

## What to listen for at each station

These are the key facts each written answer should include. Check that he **restates the
question** and answers **every part**. Don't give him the sentences; prompt with the
cheat-card question instead.

- **Station 1:** corn (Mexico), rice (China), wheat and barley (Middle East) · sheep, goats,
  dogs, cattle for loads, milk, and meat · dogs helped hunt and guard · farmed crops are
  bigger and give more food than the wild ones.
- **Station 2:** a steady food supply freed time to learn other skills · weaving, building,
  toolmaking, mining, jewelry · Standard of Ur shows soldiers, farmers, servants, a
  musician, and a king drawn larger → yes, some people were valued more.
- **Station 3:** sharper flint and obsidian tools, metal (copper, gold), woven cloth, clay
  pots · shelters went from temporary tents to permanent mud-brick houses (roof-ladder
  entry at Çatal Höyük).
- **Station 4:** walls and forts, soldiers, laws · kings rose because bigger towns had
  more crime, arguments, and outside threats; leaders kept power with advisors and myths ·
  leaders' jobs: laws, punishment, war, settling disputes, public buildings, taxes ·
  Hammurabi: harsh punishments, "eye for an eye," different punishments for different
  social classes, laws written down publicly.
- **Station 5:** temples honored gods and stored food to share in bad years · inventories
  tracked food · early writing was for records and lists of leaders · cuneiform went from
  pictures to simpler wedge marks, likely because they were faster to write in clay.

**Coaching tip:** It's a long packet. Aim for **one station a day**. When an answer is thin,
don't redo it for him. Ask "Did you restate the question? Did you answer both parts?" and
let him add to it.

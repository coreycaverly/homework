This unit is about the **Neolithic Age** (the "New Stone Age"), when people stopped
wandering and started **farming**. It looks like a lot of material, but it's really
**one big change** with five results. Do **one station a day**, about 10 minutes each.

## The whole unit, in one line

> **Farming → extra food → people settle down → jobs, tools, leaders, and writing.**

Every station is one link in that chain. If you're stuck on a question, ask:
*"How did farming cause this?"*

## Before and after: Paleolithic vs. Neolithic

| | **Paleolithic** (Old Stone Age) | **Neolithic** (New Stone Age) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Food** | hunting and gathering, always searching | farming crops and raising animals |
| **Home** | tents and rock shelters; move often | permanent mud-brick houses in villages |
| **Jobs** | almost everyone looks for food | many different jobs (weaver, builder, toolmaker) |
| **Tools** | rough stone choppers | sharp flint axes, obsidian knives, some metal |
| **Clothes** | rough animal skins | woven cloth (wool, linen, cotton) |
| **Leaders** | small groups, no kings | leaders, laws, taxes, and later kings |

## How to do a station (the 3-step method)

1. **Read with a pencil.** Box every **bold word**. Those are your vocabulary terms.
2. **Answer with ACE.** **A**nswer in a complete sentence that **restates the question** ·
   **C**ite a detail from the reading · **E**xplain how you know.
3. **STOP & CHECK** before moving to the next station (the box is at the bottom).

**Two-part questions need two answers.** If it asks *what* **and** *where*, or
*what* **and** *what for*, underline both parts and answer both.

| Weak | Strong | Clue |
| --- | --- | --- |
| *Wheat and rice.* | *Some of the earliest crops were wheat and barley in the Middle East and rice in China.* | restate the question + answer **both** parts |
| *Because of farming.* | *People settled down because farming gave them a steady food supply, so they didn't have to follow wild animals.* | never start with "Because"; explain the *how* |

## Vocabulary (7 words)

Write each definition **in your own words**. Here are kid-sized versions to check against:

| Word | It means… | Memory hook |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **cultivate** | to grow plants carefully on purpose, saving seeds from the best ones | *cultivate* ≈ "cultivate a garden" |
| **domesticate** | to tame plants or animals so they live and work with people | *domestic* = home, so animals "brought home" |
| **agriculture** | farming: growing crops **and** raising animals | *agri* = field |
| **specialization of labor** | people doing different jobs instead of everyone finding food | a *special* job for each person |
| **taxes** (also called **tribute**) | money or goods people give their ruler to pay for things everyone uses | walls, temples, roads |
| **pictograms** | picture symbols that stand for things (a drawing of grain = grain) | *picto* = picture |
| **cuneiform** | the Sumerians' writing: wedge-shaped marks pressed into wet clay | *cunei* = wedge |

## Station 1: Farming & domestication

- Hunting and gathering didn't always give enough food, and if a group stayed put, the
  plants and animals around them ran out.
- People (mostly the women and girls who gathered plants) learned they could **plant
  seeds** and grow food on purpose. They kept the seeds from the best plants: that's
  **cultivating**.
- Early crops by about 4000 B.C.E.: **corn** in Mexico · **rice** in China ·
  **wheat and barley** in the Middle East.
- Early **domesticated** animals: **sheep, goats, dogs, cattle**. Uses: carrying heavy
  loads, milk, meat, wool. **Dogs** helped hunt, guarded camps, and herded animals.
- Farming = a **reliable food supply** → people could **settle down** in one place.

**Activity tip (crop pictures):** compare the wild plant to the farmed one. Look at **size**
and **how much food** it gives. Sentence frame: *"I notice that the domesticated ___ is
___ than the early one, because farmers ___."*

## Station 2: Specialization of labor

- Farming made **extra food**, so not everyone had to farm. People had time to learn
  **new skills**.
- Early jobs: farmer · weaver (spinning wool and linen into cloth) · builder ·
  toolmaker · miner (flint and **obsidian**, a black volcanic glass) · jewelry maker.
- **Çatal Höyük** (in modern Turkey) was a wealthy early town known for these crafts.
- More jobs led to **social classes**: richer people did less hard physical work.

**Activity tip (Standard of Ur):** it's a decorated box from Sumer with a **war side** and
a **peace side**. Hunt for jobs: soldiers, chariot drivers, farmers leading animals,
servants carrying goods, a musician with a harp-like instrument, and the king.
**Big clue:** the king is drawn **bigger** than everyone else. Artists showed importance with size.

| Weak | Strong | Clue |
| --- | --- | --- |
| *Yes. The king.* | *Yes, I think some people became more valued than others. In the artwork the king is drawn bigger than everyone around him, which shows he was the most important.* | answer yes/no **and** "how do you know?" |

## Station 3: Inventions, technology & shelter

| Changed | Paleolithic | Neolithic |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Tools** | rough stone **choppers** | sharper **flint axes**, **obsidian knives**, tools of **copper** and **gold** |
| **Clothing** | rough animal skins | lighter woven cloth (**wool, cotton, silk**) |
| **Storage** | baskets and animal skins | **clay pots**: stronger and longer-lasting |
| **Shelter** | tents and rock shelters (temporary) | **permanent mud-brick houses**, round or rectangular, strengthened with stone and branches |

- In **Çatal Höyük**, people entered their houses **through the roof by ladder**, and
  houses had built-in ovens and storage.

**Activity tip (tool sort):** **rough and chipped** → Paleolithic. **Smooth, sharp, polished,
metal, or a pot** → Neolithic.

## Station 4: Trade, security & early government

- **Trade:** extra food and crafts could be traded. **Merchants** organized trade, and some
  got rich, so the gap between **rich and poor** grew.
- **Security:** people built **walls** to protect their stuff. These became **forts** and
  **citadels**. **Soldiers** became a new job.
- **Bigger towns = bigger problems:** more people needed food and houses, neighbors argued,
  and there was crime and danger from outside.
- **What leaders did:** built walls · made and enforced laws · punished lawbreakers ·
  fought wars · settled arguments · built public buildings · **collected taxes**.
- **How kings began:** leaders used **advisors** and told **myths** that made them seem
  special, so power passed down through their families. Those families became the first
  **kings and queens**.

**Activity tip (Hammurabi's Code):** this was one of the first **written** law codes, from
Babylon. When you read the examples, look for:
- How **harsh** the punishments are ("an eye for an eye").
- Whether the punishment is the **same for everyone** or depends on whether you're rich,
  poor, or enslaved.
- Why it matters that the laws were **written down** where people could see them.

Write each observation as a full sentence: *"I notice that ___. For example, ___."*

## Station 5: Writing systems

- **Sumer** was in southern **Mesopotamia** (modern Iraq). The Sumerians built huge
  **temples** for their gods.
- Temples also **stored food**. People gave food in good years, and the temple shared it
  when farmers didn't have enough.
- To keep track, they made **inventories** (lists of what they had) on **clay tablets**,
  using **pictograms**.
- Over time the pictures became simpler **wedge-shaped marks**: **cuneiform**. Symbols
  later stood for **sounds** too, so people could write more words.
- Writing wasn't only for food. One early tablet from the city of **Uruk** lists
  **important leaders**.

**Activity tip (how cuneiform changed):** compare the oldest symbols to the newest. Notice
that they get **simpler**, **less like pictures**, and sometimes **turned sideways**.
*Why?* Simple wedge marks are **faster** to press into wet clay with a reed.

| Weak | Strong | Clue |
| --- | --- | --- |
| *It changed.* | *Cuneiform changed from detailed pictures into simple wedge marks. I think this happened because simple marks were faster to press into clay.* | the question asks **what** you notice **and why** |

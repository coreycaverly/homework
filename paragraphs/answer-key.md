## Answer key (for parents)

For the practice section ("Build it") in the [study guide](guide.html).

| # | A good answer | Skill | Why |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | e.g. *"Libraries are useful for several reasons."* | Topic | Restates the question as a complete statement that previews the reasons. |
| 2 | e.g. *"Recycling saves materials, **so** we cut down fewer trees and use less energy."* | Elaborate | Adds the "so what" — why saving materials matters. |
| 3 | **Finally,** (or *Last,* / *Most importantly,*) | Connect | A "last" transition signals the final piece of evidence. |
| 4 | *"The sun gives light, which plants use to grow our food."* | Elaborate | It explains the result; "The sun gives light" is a bare fact. |
| 5 | **final transition** (e.g. *In conclusion,* / *All in all,*) | Connect | A conclusion opens with a wrap-up transition, then restates the main idea. |
| 6 | **Yes — it's strong** | Topic | A complete sentence that previews three reasons. Don't "fix" it. |

**Coaching tip (point to the step, not the mistake):** his strength is structure; the
points are in **elaboration**. When an answer stops at the evidence, ask **"so what? why
does that matter?"** and have him add one sentence starting *"This matters because…"* or
*"As a result…"*. For flow, have him read it aloud and drop a transition (First / Next /
Finally) in front of each new reason. Praise the explaining ("you told me *why* it
matters — that's the part that earns points") more than the facts.

You already know the facts. The points on a paragraph come from **building it all the
way**: a topic sentence, evidence you actually **explain**, transitions that connect
each point, and a conclusion. The one that costs the most points is **elaboration** —
explaining *why* each piece of evidence matters.

## The whole trick, in one line

> **Topic sentence → evidence + "so what?" → connect it → wrap it up → check.**

## The shape of a paragraph

1. **Topic sentence** — restate the question in a complete sentence.
2. **Evidence 1 + elaboration** — a reason, then explain *why it matters*.
3. **Evidence 2 + elaboration** — connect it with a transition.
4. **Evidence 3 + elaboration** — connect it with a transition.
5. **Conclusion** — a final transition + restate the main idea.

## 1. Topic sentence: restate the question

Start by turning the question into a statement. **Never** open with *"Because…"* or a
bare word.

| Weak | Strong |
| --- | --- |
| *Yes.* / *Because it's fun.* | *Recess is important for three reasons.* |
| Q: *How did the printing press change the world?* | *The printing press changed the world in several big ways.* |

## 2. Elaborate: the "so what?" (this is the big one)

Evidence alone isn't enough. After you state a reason, **explain why it matters** — add a
*so what?* The teacher's questions ("What did this allow? What benefit did it give?") are
asking you to **elaborate**.

| Evidence only (thin) | Evidence + elaboration (strong) |
| --- | --- |
| *Dogs can smell very well.* | *Dogs can smell very well. **This matters because** they can track a missing person by scent, something people can't do.* |
| *The printing press made books faster.* | *The printing press made books faster. **As a result,** more people could read and share ideas.* |

**Sentence starters that elaborate:** *This matters because… · This shows that… · As a
result… · This allowed people to… · In other words…*

> **Elaboration ≠ opinion.** *"In my opinion dogs are cute"* adds no reason. Explain the
> *why*, not how you feel.

## 3. Connect: transitions between your points

Use a transition to start each new piece of evidence so the paragraph flows.

| Where | Transitions to use |
| --- | --- |
| first evidence | **First,** · **To begin with,** · **One reason…** |
| next evidence | **Next,** · **Another reason…** · **In addition,** · **Also,** |
| last evidence | **Finally,** · **Last,** · **Most importantly,** |
| conclusion | **In conclusion,** · **All in all,** · **To sum up,** |

## 4. Conclude + check

End with a **final transition** and **restate the main idea** — don't add a brand-new
reason. Then run the whole thing through **CUPS+E**:

**C**apitals · **U**sage (who + did what, does it make sense) · **P**unctuation ·
**S**pelling · **+E**xpand (did I elaborate every piece of evidence?).

## Cheat card (cut out and keep by the homework)

|  | Ask yourself | Watch for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Topic** | Did I restate the question in a full sentence? | starting with "Because" or a bare word |
| **Elaborate** | After the evidence, did I say *why it matters*? | evidence with no "so what" |
| **Connect** | Does each new point start with a transition? | points jammed together, no transition |
| **Finish** | Conclusion + CUPS+E (did I **Expand** each reason)? | thin, unexplained evidence |

## STOP & CHECK (the finish box — this is the work)

You're not done until **every box is ticked**:

- ☐ A **topic sentence** that restates the question.
- ☐ **Three pieces of evidence**, each with a **"so what?"** elaboration.
- ☐ A **transition** in front of each new piece of evidence.
- ☐ A **conclusion** that starts with a final transition and restates the main idea.
- ☐ **CUPS+E:** capitals, complete sentences, punctuation, spelling, and I **Expanded** each reason.
- ☐ I **whisper-read** it and it makes sense.

**Evidence without a "so what?" loses points. Explain every reason, then connect it.**

Each week brings a new word list. The goal isn't memorizing a dictionary line. It's
knowing the word well enough to **use it in your own sentence**. Do a few words a day,
about 5 minutes.

## The whole trick, in one line

> **Say it → picture it → use it in a sentence that shows what it means.**

## How to learn a word (3 steps)

1. **Say it** out loud, and say the meaning in **your own words**.
2. **Picture it.** Think of one real thing it describes (a cat *shedding* on the couch).
3. **Use it.** Write a sentence where a reader could **guess the meaning** from the clues around it.

| Weak sentence | Strong sentence | Clue |
| --- | --- | --- |
| *I saw something continuous.* | *The continuous beeping of the alarm went on all night without a break.* | the clue "without a break" **shows** the meaning |
| *The bear will emerge.* | *After a long winter asleep, the bear began to emerge from its cave.* | add **where/when** so the meaning is clear |

## Words with more than one meaning

Many of these words have **2–4 meanings**. Read the **whole sentence**, then pick the
meaning that fits. Try swapping in each meaning: the one that makes sense wins.

> *Our dog **sheds** every spring.* → loses hair (not "gives off light")

## Word families: same root, different job

| Word | Job | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **domesticate** → **domesticated** | action → describing word | People *domesticated* wolves long ago, so dogs are *domesticated* animals. |
| **inhibit** → **inhibited** | action → describing word (shy) | Fear can *inhibit* you; an *inhibited* person holds back. |
| **motion** → **motionless** | movement → no movement | *-less* means "without": motionless = without motion. |

## Lesson 6 words

| Word | Meaning(s), in plain words | My example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **apparel** (n) | clothing; what people wear | The store sells soccer apparel like jerseys and socks. |
| **appreciate** (v) | 1. to value or be thankful for · 2. to go up in value | I appreciate the help. · Old comic books can appreciate a lot. |
| **continuous** (adj) | going on without stopping | The waterfall made a continuous roar. |
| **dissolve** (v) | 1. to mix into a liquid until you can't see it · 2. to end or break up | Salt dissolves in soup. · The band dissolved after one year. |
| **domesticate** (v) | to tame a plant or animal so it lives with people | Early farmers domesticated goats for milk. |
| **domesticated** (adj) | tamed; raised by people | Cows are domesticated animals. |
| **emerge** (v) | 1. to come out into view · 2. to become known | A turtle emerged from the pond. · The truth emerged later. |
| **fiber** (n) | 1. a thin thread used to make cloth · 2. stringy cells in muscles and nerves · 3. the part of plant food that helps you digest | Wool fibers become yarn. · Apples have fiber. |
| **function** (v / n) | (v) to work or serve a purpose · (n) 1. a purpose or job · 2. a fancy event | This bench also functions as storage. · The awards dinner was a formal function. |
| **hatch** (v / n) | (v) 1. to come out of an egg · 2. to think up a plan · (n) a small door or opening | The duck eggs hatched. · We hatched a plan. · Close the hatch! |
| **inhibit** (v) | to hold back or stop from happening | Paint inhibits rust on a fence. |
| **inhibited** (adj) | held back because of shyness | He felt inhibited speaking in front of the class. |
| **minute** (adj) | very tiny (say it *my-NOOT*) | A minute crumb fell into my keyboard. |
| **motion** (n / v) | (n) 1. movement · 2. an idea a group votes on · (v) to signal with your hand | The rocking motion of the boat · The class voted on the motion. · She motioned for me to sit. |
| **motionless** (adj) | not moving at all | The cat sat motionless, watching the bird. |
| **sheathe** (v) | to cover with something that protects | The knight sheathed his sword. |
| **shed** (v) | 1. to lose or drop (hair, skin, leaves) · 2. to let flow (tears) · 3. to let water run off · 4. to give off (light) | Snakes shed their skin. · A good jacket sheds rain. |
| **transfer** (v / n) | (v) to move from one place or person to another · (n) 1. the move itself · 2. a ticket to switch buses or trains | I transferred my notes to a new binder. · Ask the driver for a transfer. |

**History link:** *domesticate* is also a word in the [Neolithic Age](../neolithic/guide.html) lesson.

## Cheat card (cut out and keep by the homework)

| Ask yourself | Watch for |
| --- | --- |
| Can I say it in **my own words**? | don't just copy the dictionary line |
| Does it have **more than one meaning**? | shed · hatch · minute · motion · function · fiber · dissolve · appreciate |
| Is it the **action** or the **describing** form? | domesticate/domesticated · inhibit/inhibited · motion/motionless |
| Does my sentence **show** the meaning? | add a clue: where, when, how, or why |

## STOP & CHECK (the finish box: this is the work)

You're not done until **every box is ticked**:

- ☐ I can say every word's meaning **in my own words**.
- ☐ For words with several meanings, I know **at least two**.
- ☐ Each sentence I wrote is **complete** (who + did what) and **shows** the meaning.
- ☐ I **whisper-read** my sentences, and they make sense.
- ☐ I checked off every activity on the weekly tracker.

**A few words a day, checked and finished, beats the whole list rushed.**

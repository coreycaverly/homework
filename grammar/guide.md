## Start here: the 3-pass method

Do one section a day, about 5 minutes each. Set a timer, then stop when it rings, even if you're on a roll.

**Your mission on every Editor in Chief passage:** find every error the circles say are hiding. The circles are your checklist. You're not done until every circle is crossed off.

1. **Count first.** Look at the circles. Write the totals in the margin, like **ADJ 4 · ADV 5 · ART 2**.
2. **Pass 1: Adjectives.** Read the passage once and look only at words that describe nouns. Cross off a circle for each fix.
3. **Pass 2: Adverbs.** Read it again and look only at words that answer *how, when, where,* or *how much*.
4. **Pass 3: Articles.** Read it one last time and look only at *a, an,* and *the*.
5. **Read it out loud.** Whisper-read the fixed passage. If a sentence sounds silly or doesn't make sense, an error is still hiding there.

**Brain tip:** Put your finger or a pencil under each word as you read. Take a 30-second stretch break after each pass. Paragraph 2 counts just as much as paragraph 1.

## Adjectives: they describe NOUNS

An adjective tells you about a person, place, or thing. It answers **What kind? Which one? How many?**

> *the* **sharp** *teeth* → What kind of teeth? Sharp.

### Trap 1: A noun pretending to be an adjective

If a word sits right before a noun but is itself a *thing*, it probably needs an ending like **-ful, -y, -ed, -ous**.

| Wrong | Right | Clue |
| --- | --- | --- |
| its **power** hind legs | its **powerful** hind legs | power is a thing; powerful describes |
| a **health** change | a **healthy** change | add **-y** |
| our **thatch** hut | our **thatched** hut | add **-ed** |

### Trap 2: After "is, was, were, seems, smells" use an ADJECTIVE

These verbs work like an equals sign. The word after them describes the noun, so it's an adjective (no -ly).

- The teeth **were effective**. *(teeth = effective)*
- The soup **smells wonderful**. *(not wonderfully)*

### Trap 3: Comparing (count how many!)

| Comparing... | Short words | Long words | Example |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **2 things** | add **-er** | use **more** | taller, more feared |
| **3 or more** | add **-est** | use **most** | tallest of all, most feared |

Rule of thumb: **"of all" or "ever" means -est / most.**

### Trap 4: The rule-breakers

| One | Two (-er) | All (-est) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| good | **better** | **best** |
| bad | **worse** | **worst** |
| many / much | **more** | **most** |

**Make-sense check:** "Our *better* ones are not as good as your *worse* ones." Aunt is comparing her whole pile to yours, so it's **best** and **worst**. Ask: *Is this comparing 2, or comparing the top/bottom of a group?*

## Adverbs: the 4 questions (not just -ly!)

**Big idea:** An adverb is NOT "a word that ends in -ly." An adverb is any word that answers one of these 4 questions about a verb, an adjective, or another adverb:

| Question | Example adverbs | In a sentence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **HOW?** | quickly, well, fast, certainly | It ran **fast**. |
| **WHEN?** | yesterday, tomorrow, soon, now, always | **Yesterday**, Auntie called. |
| **WHERE?** | there, here, back, anywhere | We spent time **there**. |
| **HOW MUCH?** | very, enough, approximately, intensely | **intensely** feared |

### Trap 1: Missing -ly

If a word describes *how* something is done, and it has an -ly form, use it.

- as it is **popular** called → **popularly** called
- will **certain** have to wait → will **certainly** have to wait
- an **intense** feared predator → an **intensely** feared predator *("how feared?")*

### Trap 2: Fake -ly (an extra -ly that doesn't belong)

Some adverbs **never** take -ly. If you see one of these with -ly stuck on, it's an error.

**No -ly words:** enough · fast · hard · soon · late · there · here · yesterday · tomorrow · always

- **enoughly** time → **enough** time
- ran **fastly** → ran **fast**

### Trap 3: The wrong WHEN word

Time words have to match the verb's time. Read the sentence and ask: *Did this already happen, or is it going to happen?*

- ~~Tomorrow~~, Auntie **called** → **Yesterday**, Auntie called. *(called = already happened)*

### Trap 4: good vs. well

**Good** describes a noun (adjective). **Well** describes how something is done (adverb).

- She is a **good** swimmer. / She swims **well**.

## Articles: a, an, the

There are only 3 articles. That makes them the easiest points on the page.

### Rule 1: a vs. an goes by SOUND, not spelling

Say the next word out loud. Vowel **sound** (a, e, i, o, u sound) → **an**. Consonant sound → **a**.

| Word | Sounds like | Article |
| --- | --- | --- |
| honor | "ON-er" (silent h) | **an** honor |
| hour | "OW-er" | **an** hour |
| unicorn | "YOU-nicorn" | **a** unicorn |
| tree | "tree" | **a** tree |
| intensely feared | "IN-tensely" | **an** intensely feared |

**Watch out:** the article matches the word right after it, even if that word is an adjective. *an* **o**ld dog, but *a* **b**ig old dog.

### Rule 2: a vs. the

- **a / an** = any one, not a specific one. *"the fruit of* **a** *tree called the coconut palm"*
- **the** = a specific one, or the only one. *"the ocean," "the king of them all," "the moon"*

**Quick test:** Is there only one, or do we already know which one? → **the**.

## Practice: spot it, fix it

Circle the wrong word and write the fix on the line. Each sentence has **one** error, except one sentence that is a trick.

### Round 1: the error type is given

1. **\[ADVERB\]** The puppy ran quick across the yard. Fix: \_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_
2. **\[ADJECTIVE\]** She is the faster runner on the whole team. Fix: \_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_
3. **\[ARTICLE\]** We saw a elephant at the zoo. Fix: \_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_
4. **\[ADJECTIVE\]** It was a danger storm, so we stayed inside. Fix: \_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_
5. **\[ADVERB\]** I didn't have enoughly money for a snack. Fix: \_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_
6. **\[ADVERB\]** Tomorrow we went to the beach and built a sandcastle. Fix: \_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_

*30-second stretch break!*

### Round 2: detective mode (no hints)

Write **ADJ, ADV,** or **ART** after each fix.

7. Mia did good on her spelling test. Fix: \_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_ Type: \_\_\_\_
8. Of the two pies, the cherry one is the best. Fix: \_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_ Type: \_\_\_\_
9. The moon is a brightest object in the night sky. Fix: \_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_ Type: \_\_\_\_
10. The garbage smelled terribly. Fix: \_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_ Type: \_\_\_\_
11. That was a extremely loud thunderclap. Fix: \_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_ Type: \_\_\_\_
12. This is the worse movie I have ever seen. Fix: \_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_ Type: \_\_\_\_
13. We waited an hour for the bus. Fix: \_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_ Type: \_\_\_\_

## Cheat card (cut out and keep by the homework)

|  | Ask yourself | Watch for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **ADJECTIVE** | What kind? Which one? How many? | noun used instead of adjective (power → powerful) · after is/was/were · 2 = -er, 3+ = -est · good/better/best, bad/worse/worst |
| **ADVERB** | How? When? Where? How much? | missing -ly · fake -ly (enoughly) · wrong time word (Tomorrow... called) · good vs. well |
| **ARTICLE** | Does the next word start with a vowel SOUND? Is it a specific one? | a honor → an honor · a elephant → an elephant · a vs. the |

**Not done until every circle is crossed off. Read it out loud at the end.**

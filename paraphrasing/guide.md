Paraphrasing means putting someone else's idea into **your own words**. The trap is easy
to fall into: you keep most of the original's words and leave them in the same order. A
real paraphrase uses **new words** AND a **new order** — and still means the same thing.

## The whole trick, in one line

> **Read it, look away, say it your way — then check it still means the same.**

Read the original, look away so you can't copy it, and say the idea like you'd tell a
friend. Then reread: new words? new order? same meaning?

## PARA: the four-step check

| | Step | |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **P** | **Put** it in your own words | say it like you'd explain it out loud |
| **A** | **Avoid** copying | don't keep the original's key words |
| **R** | **Rearrange** | change the order of the ideas / the sentence shape |
| **A** | **Ask** | did I keep the important parts and the same meaning? |

## The three moves of a strong paraphrase

### 1. New words (swap in synonyms)
Trade the key words for words that mean the same. **Changing one word and keeping the
rest is still copying.**

| Too close (copying) | Real paraphrase |
| --- | --- |
| *The enormous whale dove into the deep ocean.* → *The enormous whale dove into the deep sea.* | *The giant whale plunged far below the surface.* |

> **Careful:** don't swap *every* word for a fancy synonym — it gets awkward. Swap the
> ones that matter and keep it sounding like you.

### 2. New order (switch it up)
Don't leave the ideas in the same order you found them. Flip the sentence around.

| Same order | Reordered |
| --- | --- |
| *Because it rained, the game was canceled.* | *The game was called off, since it had rained.* |

### 3. Keep the main idea (you don't need every detail)
You don't have to include every tiny detail — keep the **important** parts and the
**meaning**. Trimming a small detail is fine; changing the meaning is not.

## Watch-outs (the mistakes that cost points)

| Wrong | Right | Clue |
| --- | --- | --- |
| copies key words, same order | new words, new order | PARA: **A**void copying, **R**earrange |
| *Because he gave a speech in the rain.* (fragment) | *He got sick because he gave a speech in the rain.* | add a **who + did what** |
| *The storm hit the town it knocked down trees.* (run-on) | *The storm hit the town. It knocked down trees.* | split, or join with **, and** |
| *The dogs wagged **there** tails.* | *The dogs wagged **their** tails.* | *their* = they own it |

## Practice: fix the paraphrase

Each one paraphrases the same idea. Write a better version (or mark it "already good").

1. **\[Own words\]** Original: *"The volcano erupted suddenly."* → Paraphrase: *"The volcano erupted fast."* \_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_
2. **\[Order\]** Original: *"After the bell rang, students left class."* → reorder it. \_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_
3. **\[Check\]** Fix: *"Because the bridge was old."* \_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_
4. **\[Check\]** Fix the run-on: *"The team practiced hard they won the game."* \_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_
5. **\[Own words\]** Trick — is this already a good paraphrase? Original: *"Bees move pollen between flowers."* → *"Bees carry pollen from flower to flower."* \_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_
6. **\[Check\]** Choose: *"The cats chased \_\_\_ toy."* (there / their) \_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_

## Cheat card (cut out and keep by the homework)

|  | Ask yourself | Watch for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Own words** | Did I swap the key words for synonyms? | keeping the original's words; changing just one |
| **New order** | Did I flip the sentence / reorder the ideas? | same order as the original |
| **Keep & check** | Same meaning? Important parts kept? | fragments, run-ons, homophones, changed meaning |

## STOP & CHECK (the finish box — this is the work)

You're not done until **every box is ticked**:

- ☐ I read the original, looked away, and wrote it **from memory**.
- ☐ I used **my own words** — the key words are swapped, not copied.
- ☐ I **changed the order** — it's not the original with a few words traded.
- ☐ It still **means the same thing**, with the important parts kept.
- ☐ Every sentence is **complete** (who + did what) — no fragments, no run-ons.
- ☐ I checked **homophones** (there/their, to/too/two).

**A paraphrase that's "too similar" loses points. New words, new order, same meaning.**

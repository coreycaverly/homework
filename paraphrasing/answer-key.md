## Answer key (for parents)

For the practice section ("Fix the paraphrase") in the [study guide](guide.html).

| # | A good answer | Skill | Why |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | e.g. *"The volcano blew up without warning."* | Own words | "erupted fast" just swaps one word. Reword the key words (erupted→blew up, suddenly→without warning). |
| 2 | e.g. *"Students left class once the bell rang."* | Order | Puts the leaving first and the bell second — the order is switched, not copied. |
| 3 | e.g. *"The old bridge finally gave way."* | Keep & check | *"Because the bridge was old"* is a fragment — add a who + did what. |
| 4 | **"The team practiced hard, so they won the game."** | Keep & check | Run-on — split with a period or join with ", so". |
| 5 | **Already good** | Own words | New words (help→carry) and new order, same meaning. Don't "fix" a strong paraphrase. |
| 6 | **their** | Keep & check | *their* = they own it (the cats' toy). |

**Coaching tip (point to the step, not the mistake):** when a paraphrase is "too similar,"
don't rewrite it for him — ask "which **key words** can you swap?" and "can you say the
**second** idea first?" Have him read the original, then **flip the page over** and write
from memory — that one habit kills most copying. Praise the reordering ("you started with
a different idea — that's a real paraphrase") as much as the synonyms.

## Start here: say it back

For science, the trick isn't finding a mistake — it's **explaining why**. After you
read a section:

1. **Read** the short rule.
2. **Say it back** out loud in your own words, like you're teaching it.
3. **Name the equation or law** that goes with it (F = m × a? 1st, 2nd, or 3rd law?).
4. **Then** try the practice question.

**Brain tip:** finger under each line, a 30-second stretch between sections, and when
you answer, always add *"because…"* with the law or the formula.

## Describe motion first

| Word | What it really means | Watch out |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Speed** | how fast (just a number) | 30 mph |
| **Velocity** | speed **AND direction** | 30 mph *east* |
| **Acceleration** | **any change** in velocity | speeding up, slowing down, **or turning** |
| **Inertia** | an object's resistance to a change in motion | **more mass = more inertia** |

> A car going a steady 30 mph around a curve **is accelerating** — its *direction* is
> changing, so its velocity is changing.

## Newton's three laws (in kid words)

| Law | Kid version | Clue words |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **1st — Inertia** | Things keep doing what they're doing until a force changes it. | "stays still," "keeps moving," "until…" |
| **2nd — F = m × a** | Force equals mass times acceleration. | a number problem, "how much," "accelerates more" |
| **3rd — Action/Reaction** | Every push has an equal and opposite push back. | "pushes back," "two objects," rocket, swimmer |

### Trap 1: More mass means LESS acceleration (same force)

This is the one to nail. With the **same push**, the **lighter** thing speeds up more.

| Wrong | Right | Clue |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Same push → the **full** cart speeds up more | Same push → the **empty** cart speeds up more | a = F ÷ m. Less mass, more acceleration. |

> **Say it back:** "Same force. The empty cart has less mass, so it accelerates more,
> because F = m × a."

### Trap 2: Why action–reaction pairs don't cancel

The two forces are equal and opposite — so why doesn't everything freeze?

| Wrong | Right | Clue |
| --- | --- | --- |
| They cancel because they push on each other | They act on **two different objects**, so they can't cancel on one | Foot pushes ground; ground pushes foot — different objects. |

## Do the math

**F = m × a** — three steps, every time:

1. **Write the formula:** F = m × a
2. **Substitute:** F = 6 kg × 4 m/s²
3. **Solve + units:** F = **24 N**

Rearrange it when they ask for something else:

- Need acceleration? **a = F ÷ m**
- Need mass? **m = F ÷ a**

**Net force** (add up the forces):

- Same direction → **add** them.
- Opposite directions → **subtract**, and the answer points the bigger way.
- Equal and opposite → **0 N (balanced)** → no change in motion.

**Momentum: p = m × v.** A 1,000 kg car at 20 m/s has p = 20,000 kg·m/s.

## Balanced vs. unbalanced

- **Balanced** forces → net force **0 N** → motion **doesn't change**.
- **Unbalanced** forces → net force **not zero** → the object **accelerates**.

## Cheat card (cut out and keep by the homework)

|  | Ask yourself | Watch for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Motion** | Speed, or speed **and direction**? Is anything changing? | velocity = +direction · turning still = accelerating · more mass = more inertia |
| **Newton's Laws** | 1st (inertia), 2nd (F=ma), or 3rd (push-back)? | same force → lighter accelerates more · pairs act on **different objects** |
| **Calculations** | What's the formula? Plug in, then units. | F = m×a · a = F÷m · net force adds/subtracts · p = m×v |
